03

Objectives

This course builds vocabulary and public English usage through popular TED Talks, celebrity interviews, and documentaries. Over the semester you will:

  • Improve speaking and listening. Follow an argument delivered at speed, and answer it.
  • Learn practical colloquial English. The expressions people actually use, not only the ones in textbooks.
  • Build confidence in short speeches. Stand up, speak for a few minutes, and be understood.
  • Understand American and British cultures. The context that makes an argument land, or fail to.
04

Every Meeting

The same three moves, in the same order, every week. You will always know what is coming, so you can spend your attention on the English rather than on the schedule.

Move 1

Watch

  • The week's talk, interview, or documentary clip.
  • Some weeks the viewing is set before class. Those are marked below.
  • Take notes on the claim, the evidence, and the words you did not have.
Move 2

Analyse

  • Work out causes and effects, not just the topic.
  • Name the rhetorical moves the speaker uses on you.
  • Decide whether the evidence actually supports the claim.
Move 3

Speak

  • Discuss, debate, or propose a solution, in English.
  • Use the new vocabulary out loud while it is still fresh.
  • This is the rehearsal for your midterm and your capstone.
05

Requirements and Grading

You are expected to take an active part in class: watching the clips, debating, presenting in English, and taking notes. Participation is not a bonus here, it is a fifth of the grade.

Breakdown 100% plus 5% bonus
20%

Oral Recordings

Record yourself speaking for about three minutes on the material covered in class. Five recordings in total, each worth 4%. Each one should show real engagement with the talk or documentary, not a summary read aloud.

30%

Midterm Essay

Submit and present a proposal for your capstone. State your topic, explain why it belongs in this course, and set out a preliminary plan for your research or analysis.

30%

Capstone Presentation

In pairs or groups of three, choose a topic related to the course and deliver a final presentation in English that pulls your research or analysis together. Slides and other aids are welcome.

20%

Attendance and Discussion

Being in the room, and speaking while you are in it.

+5%

Final Reflective Essay (optional bonus)

Reflect on your semester: what you learned, what you enjoyed, and how the capstone changed your understanding or your skills. Worth up to 5 bonus points on your final grade.

07

Class Rules and Policies

Attendance

Unexcused absences are cumulative:

  • First unexcused absence: 10% deducted from your total score.
  • Second: a further 10% deducted.
  • Third: you fail the course.

To avoid these penalties, report the absence through the school system and email me at least 24 hours before the class, with valid documentation.

Plagiarism and generative AI

Plagiarism results in failure of this course.

AI-generated content may not be used in essays, writing assignments, or speeches for this course. Submitting AI-generated content counts as academic dishonesty: it means an automatic failure of that assignment, and repeated cases can mean failure of the course. Your work must begin as your own writing, not as an AI draft you then edit.

For the university guidelines on generative AI and AI-assisted technologies, see english.fju.edu.tw/faculty_area_detail.asp?OFT_No=34.
