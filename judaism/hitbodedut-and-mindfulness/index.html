The Question and Roadmap

Presentation 1: against Persico’s skepticism, Weil argues that Judaism has its own meditative tradition, hitbodedut, practised “since at least the 12th century, possibly much earlier.” Today we set two meditative traditions side by side, and neither is the yardstick for the other.

Question: Are hitbodedut and Thích Nhất Hạnh’s mindfulness the same kind of practice, or do they only look alike?

3

Hitbodedut

How Hitbodedut Is Practised

AddresseeGod, spoken to directly. When it goes well, “it is like a person talking to his friend” (LM II:99).
LanguageOne’s mother tongue (Yiddish, for Nachman’s Hasidim), not liturgical Hebrew (LM II:25).
ContentEverything: thoughts, needs, failures, frustrations. Nothing is too mundane.
TimeIdeally at night, when the world is asleep (LM I:52); commonly at least an hour a day.
PlaceAlone, in a room or, best, in fields and forests.
StatusA supplement to the three fixed daily services, not a replacement.

“When a person meditates in the fields, all the grasses join in his prayer and increase its effectiveness and power.”Likutei Moharan II:11, as translated in common English renderings

LM = Likutei Moharan. LM I:52; II:11; II:25; II:99. Greenbaum, The Essential Rabbi Nachman.

Speaker notes2:00 · cum. 4:00
  • Stress how unusual this is in a liturgical religion: the fixed prayers are in Hebrew and set; hitbodedut is improvised and in the vernacular.
  • The “one hour a day” guideline is standard in Breslov teaching; sources attach it to different sections of LM II, so I do not tie it to one section number.

Hitbodedut

The Goal in Likutei Moharan I:52: Bittul1

Go out aloneat night, on a path no one uses, free of the day’s distractions
→
Pour out the heartand let go of one desire or trait at a time, night after night
→
Bittulthe self is nullified and “encompassed in its Source”: God, the one necessary existent

Nachman’s argument: only God exists necessarily; the world depends on God. To return to that Source one must nullify the self, and the only way to bittul is hitbodedut.

  1. 1Bittul בִּטּוּל /biˈtul/: “nullification”; in Hasidic thought, the self dissolving before, or into, God.

LM I:52:1–4 (Sefaria); Kramer’s note to LM II:25.

Speaker notes1:30 · cum. 5:30
  • LM I:52 opens with Mishnah Avot 3:4 (one who is awake at night and walks alone) and turns its warning into an instruction.
  • Keep this slide in mind: the goal is theological. The self is not simply seen through; it is absorbed into a Being who really exists. We will compare this with interbeing on Slide 12.

Mindfulness

Thích Nhất Hạnh: Teacher and Texts

  • 1926Born in Thừa Thiên (Huế region); later a novice at Từ Hiếu Temple
  • 1960sFounds the School of Youth for Social Service, an expression of “engaged Buddhism”
  • 1966First members of the Order of Interbeing (Tiếp Hiện) ordained; exiled after calling for peace abroad
  • 1974–75Phép lạ của sự tỉnh thức, written as a letter to Brother Quang of the School; English: The Miracle of Mindfulness
  • 1982Plum Village founded in the Dordogne, France
  • 2022Dies at Từ Hiếu Temple, Huế, aged 95

The manual was meant to keep young social workers from burning out amid the war. Mindfulness here begins as survival for people doing relief work, not as private calm.

Plum Village, “The Miracle of Mindfulness”; Ho, preface to The Miracle of Mindfulness (1987 ed.).

Speaker notes1:30 · cum. 7:00
  • Date caution: the translator’s preface says the text began as a letter in 1974; Plum Village’s book page says he wrote it in 1975. I say “1974 letter, 1975 manual.”
  • Both teachers wrote under pressure: Nachman in Ukraine during the Napoleonic era, Thích Nhất Hạnh for a community in a war zone.

Mindfulness

How Mindfulness Is Practised

BreathConscious breathing as the anchor that brings mind and body back together.
Gathas1Short verses said silently while waking, walking, eating, answering the phone.
Daily tasksWashing dishes, drinking tea and walking become the practice itself.
Walking meditationEach step taken with full awareness.
Sangha2Practice is sustained by a community, lay and monastic.
EthicsThe mindfulness trainings of the Order of Interbeing guide conduct.

“…wash the dishes in order to wash the dishes.”Thích Nhất Hạnh, The Miracle of Mindfulness, ch. 1

  1. 1Gatha /ˈɡɑːtə/: a short verse (Sanskrit/Pali gāthā).
  2. 2Sangha /ˈsʌŋɡə/: the community of practitioners.

Nhat Hanh, Miracle, ch. 1; Being Peace.

Speaker notes2:00 · cum. 10:30
  • He contrasts two ways of washing dishes: to get clean dishes, or to wash the dishes. Only the second is mindfulness.
  • Note for later: a gatha is a short verbal formula. Mindfulness is not wordless.
8

Mindfulness

The Ground: Interbeing (Tiếp Hiện)

Tiếp

“being in touch with” and “continuing”

Hiện

“realizing” and “making it here and now”

“Interbeing” (French interêtre) is Thích Nhất Hạnh’s coinage for three classical Buddhist teachings:

  • anattā: no permanent, separate self
  • pratītyasamutpāda: everything arises in dependence on other things
  • śūnyatā: emptiness of independent existence

Glosses of Tiếp and Hiện: Wikipedia, “Order of Interbeing” and “Interbeing”; Nhat Hanh, Being Peace.

Speaker notes1:30 · cum. 12:00
  • His familiar example: look deeply at a sheet of paper and you see the cloud, the rain and the tree in it. The paper “inter-is.”
  • Crucial for the comparison: interbeing has no creator. Thích Nhất Hạnh does speak of an ultimate dimension (the water beneath the waves, nirvana), but not as a Being to merge into; see Slides 9 and 12.

Comparison

Weil’s Three-Step Test, Applied to Both

Weil’s step (Table 1)Hitbodedut: Nachman, LM 52 (Weil, Table 2)Thích Nhất Hạnh’s mindfulness
1. Quieting the senses“totally or partially quieting the sensitive soul” Fits“a special place and time” … “at night” … “on a secluded road” PartlyŚamatha, “stopping, calming”; guarding the six senses (Fifth Training). But it calms reactivity: the senses stay open
2. Directing the awareness“reorienting it toward God” Fits“empty his heart”; “a bitul of all corporeality” PartlyAttention is gathered and redirected, but to the present moment, not away from the world
3. Concentration on God“filling the rational soul with God” Fits“encompassed in the oneness of God” PartlyConcentration, yes; at depth it touches nirvana, which he likens to God as “ground of being.” But no Person to cleave to, and the true self “is non-self”

Looked at closely, each of Weil’s steps has a Buddhist counterpart, even a “ground of being.” What differs is the relation: Nachman cleaves to a God he can address; Thích Nhất Hạnh touches a ground no concept captures.

Step names and Nachman quotations adapted from Weil (2024), Tables 1–2, CC BY 4.0. Right column: the presenter’s application, not Weil’s.

Weil 2024, pp. 6–10; LM I:52; Nhat Hanh, Heart, ch. 6; Plum Village, Five Trainings; Lefebure on Going Home; McLeod interview. See Presentation 1, Slides 9 and 12.

Speaker notes1:30 · cum. 13:30
  • Callback to Presentation 1: this is the same yardstick Weil used to link six Jewish teachers. Weil himself placed Nachman’s Likutei Moharan 52 against it, so the middle column is his evidence, quoted as he quotes it.
  • The right column is my application; Weil does not apply his test to Thích Nhất Hạnh.
  • Two caveats. First, Weil notes (following Idel) that hitbodedut does not always require physical seclusion (p. 7), so step 1 is soft even on the Jewish side. Second, the test fits LM 52 well but misses the other face of hitbodedut: speaking to God “like a person talking to his friend” (LM II:99; cf. II:25). The template comes from Maimoni’s silent concentration, not from Nachman’s speech.
  • Step 1: Thích Nhất Hạnh calls the first aspect of meditation śamatha: stopping, calming, resting, healing (Heart of the Buddha’s Teaching, ch. 6). The Fifth Mindfulness Training asks us to guard what we take in through the six senses. But Maimoni quiets the “sensitive soul” itself (medieval faculty psychology: seclusion, night, darkness); Thích Nhất Hạnh keeps the senses open and stops being carried away by them. Same direction, different mechanism.
  • Step 3: concentration is central for him (calm first, then a one-pointed mind looks deeply). And he himself builds the bridge to God: in Going Home he takes Tillich’s God as “ground of being” as equivalent to nirvana, the water beneath the waves; the wave discovers it is water.
  • But two limits. (1) His “true self” is not a Self that is God: “True self is non-self, the awareness that the self is made only of non-self elements” (2006 interview). (2) Weil’s step 3 is cleaving (devekut) to a God who can be addressed and may grant inspiration; the ground of being is touched, not spoken to. That is the same line as Slide 11: who is addressed.
  • Caution: the God-bridge is Thích Nhất Hạnh’s own interfaith reading, offered to Christians. It is evidence of how he compares, not proof that the two practices aim at the same reality.

Comparison

Speech and Silence Are Not Strict Opposites

Hitbodedut becomes almost wordless

  • If you can say only one word, repeat it over and over (LM II:96).
  • The “silent scream”: shouting inwardly, in a still, small voice, which Nachman practised himself.

Mindfulness uses words

  • Gathas give each daily action a short verse.
  • Chanting and sutra recitation remain part of Plum Village practice.

The real difference is not speech against silence but who is addressed. A repeated word in hitbodedut is still said to someone.

LM II:96 (Greenbaum’s numbering); Sichot HaRan 16 (silent scream); Nhat Hanh, Miracle.

Speaker notes1:30 · cum. 17:30
  • It is tempting to call both a “mantra.” The form is similar; the addressee is not.

Comparison

Bittul and Interbeing: Similar on the Surface

Bittul

The self is nullified into God, the only necessary existent. Something ultimately real remains: the Source.

Interbeing

The separate self is seen to be empty. Nothing needs to merge: the wave discovers it is already water (nirvana, the “ground of being”). No creator, no Person.

Both let go of the separate self and reach a ground. They disagree about what that ground is: a God who exists necessarily and can be addressed, or a nirvana that no concept captures.

LM I:52; Nhat Hanh, Being Peace; Lefebure on Going Home.

Speaker notes1:30 · cum. 19:00
  • Some Hasidic thinkers push bittul toward a near-monism (only God truly exists), which sounds close to Thích Nhất Hạnh’s wave and water. The difference: in bittul the self must be nullified before the One who exists necessarily; in interbeing the wave only has to realize what it already is. And Nachman’s God is spoken to; the water is not.

Comparison

What to Do with Pain

Nachman

  • “It is a great mitzvah to always be happy” (LM II:24).
  • A broken heart (לב נשבר) is precious, but keep it to a set time; depression (עצבות) is to be pushed away.
  • Hitbodedut is that set time: be heartbroken there, joyful the rest of the day.

Thích Nhất Hạnh

  • Do not suppress anger or sorrow: recognize it and hold it, as a mother holds a crying baby.
  • Suffering, looked at deeply, becomes the material of understanding and compassion.

Neither practice suppresses emotion. Both give it a container: a scheduled hour before God, or a mindful embrace in the present.

LM II:24; Sichot HaRan 41–42 (numbering varies); Nhat Hanh, Anger (2001).

Speaker notes1:30 · cum. 20:30
  • Nachman struggled with deep despair himself (Green), which makes the line between heartbreak and depression personal, not abstract.
  • Thích Nhất Hạnh’s readers were relief workers witnessing war. Both practices were built for people under real strain.
14

Convergences

Where They Meet

  1. The ordinary becomes holy. Nothing is too small to tell God; nothing is too small to do mindfully.
  2. Open to everyone. “From great to small,” no one becomes truly religious without hitbodedut (LM II:99); Thích Nhất Hạnh built a lay order and lay practice.
  3. A supplement, not a replacement. Hitbodedut sits beside the siddur; Plum Village keeps its chanting and liturgy.
  4. Born under pressure. Both answer suffering rather than escaping it.

LM II:99 (Sefaria); Plum Village, “The Miracle of Mindfulness.”

Speaker notes1:30 · cum. 22:00
  • These are shared attitudes, not a shared essence: they say how each practice relates to daily life, not what the practitioner finally encounters.
15

Conclusion

Conclusion and Discussion

A shared attitude, different beliefs about God and the self. One practice speaks to a You; the other wakes up to the present moment.

  1. Can hitbodedut be practised without belief in God? What would remain?
  2. Is a gatha closer to prayer or to meditation?
  3. Who should decide which label fits a practice: insiders or scholars?
