3 · Affective ROIs — PleasantOther (gated LOSO)

Interpretation. On Payload (dialogue-dense), real LOSO means sit above their own shift-nulls → descriptive DECODES. On Tears of Steel (visual / dialogue-light), real sits inside or below an elevated null → null. Positive controls pass on all ROIs; this is not “decoder broken.” Film character is part of the finding.

4 · Multi-item brain hit rates

Interpretation. Payload: majority of tested items clear the dual gate in affective ROIs (~60–72%). Tears of Steel: ~6–17%. Expanding beyond PleasantOther shows bodily/appraisal targets (Muscle, Urgency, Surprise…) often rival valence — continuous annotation is multi-dimensional in cortex too.

7 · Physio (subject-mean) top |r|

Interpretation. Respiration (RESP) dominates top |r| against several consensus series (often arousal-related). HR/EDA are mixed and film-dependent. Treat as convergent peripheral evidence, not a second brain arm. Sample is the same 5 subjects × 2 films as the BOLD subset.

8 · Multimodal snapshot (Tears of Steel)

Interpretation. On ToS, text still owns valence (Gemini PleasantOther high positive); audio energy tracks arousal-like structure (IntenseEmotion up, Calm down); visual motion aligns with fear/anxiety-like series. Channels are complementary, not redundant. Payload audio missing until experiment cuts are restored.

9 · Annotation dynamics (top variance)

Interpretation. PleasantOther is among the most dynamic items — justifying its historical use as primary valence — but Fear, Anxiety, Stomach, Urgency, etc. are comparably variable. Median autocorrelation 1/e lags of ~30–55 s on short films motivate effect-size reporting and film-block inference, not second-level NHST stars.
